01

Evidence has a scope

The old question was “what is our verdict on this brand?” That produced errors in both directions: a pan inherited a pass because its brand was rated good, and a plain stainless skillet inherited a caution because the brand’s appliances were the problem.

The right question is what evidence do we hold, and does its scope contain the thing the shopper is looking at? Every finding attaches at one of these levels.

  • lotOne batch or production run. A contaminated lot of a botanical.
  • skuOne listing, size or flavour. Boka Ela Mint 4oz.
  • lineA named product line. Colgate Total.
  • formulaA recipe base shared across variants. A whole hydroxyapatite range.
  • formatA packaging format or construction. Their 12oz glass jar.
  • brandThe company and its conduct. Litigation over labelling claims.
  • classA material across the industry. All bentonite clay oral products.

Adverse evidence may propagate. Favourable evidence never does.

A detection on one SKU is reason to look harder at its siblings. A clean result on one SKU says nothing about siblings, because we did not test them. That asymmetry is safe in both directions: we never wrongly recommend, and when we warn we always disclose what the warning rests on.

02

The four checks

Formula — what it is made of

Judged from the ingredient list, or the material list, for that specific SKU. It transfers across sizes and multipacks, never across flavours or variants, because whitening, kids and sensitive versions routinely differ in exactly the ingredient that matters.

The line between the three answers is where the hazard sits, not whether one is mentioned:

VerdictWhat it takesExample
Good Every material in the contact path is named and inert, or the full ingredient list is published and carries nothing on the hazard list. 18/8 stainless with no coating. Six named ingredients, no fragrance.
Careful A hazard is present but bounded: outside the contact path, or the least bad option in a category with no clean one. Also here: the maker does not publish a complete list, which is a finding rather than an absence. A polypropylene shell that never touches the milk. Nylon bristles, where boar is the only plastic free option.
Skip An un-negated hazard sits in the path that reaches a person: swallowed, absorbed, inhaled, or in contact with food. PTFE on a pan. Undisclosed "gum base". Fragrance in a leave-on lotion.

What is on the hazard list

A substance earns a place only when all three hold. Route: a documented path from this product into a person — migration, shedding, absorption, inhalation, ingestion. Evidence: independent published evidence of harm, or a restriction by a serious regulator. Determinable: we can tell it is there from the label, the material or a published test, because a rule we cannot apply is not a rule.

The list then holds two kinds of entry, and they do not behave the same way.

KindWhat it isCeiling on its own
Named hazard A specific substance with evidence behind it: PTFE and the other PFAS, PVC, polycarbonate and BPA, polystyrene, melamine, phthalates, formaldehyde releasers, triclosan, lead, cadmium. Skip
Disclosure failure A legal umbrella that hides composition: fragrance, parfum, gum base, proprietary blend, natural flavors. It names no harmful substance. Careful

So is fragrance a hazard? It is on the list, but as the second kind. The objection is not to scent. "Fragrance" is one word standing in for a mixture that may run to hundreds of ingredients and has historically carried phthalates, and the word exists to make that legal. What it tells us is that we cannot check.

A disclosure failure caps at careful on its own. It is not evidence of harm — it is the absence of the evidence we would need, and the maker controls that absence, which is why it counts against them where a missing lab test does not.

That is the whole difference between this and the rule above. We do not mark a product down because no lab has looked at it; the maker does not control that. We do mark down concealment, because they chose it. Exposure then modulates the weight by the same route rule as packaging: fragrance left on skin all day is a different finding from fragrance rinsed down a drain. No product on this site is a skip for fragrance alone — every skip that mentions it carries a second, named finding beside it, and that is the rule rather than a coincidence.

A term the copy explicitly rules out — "BPA free", "no PVC", "fragrance free" — is not a hazard present.

Conditional failures

Some failures are not a property of the product as sold. They depend on how it is used, they are avoidable by stated care, and nothing about the object tells you whether they have happened.

Sophie la Girafe is the case. The rubber and the food grade paint are fine; the sealed interior cavity is the problem, because water enters during washing and cannot be dried, and mould colonies inside the toy are documented from 2017 onward. But mould is not guaranteed. It depends entirely on whether water gets in. Run that against the three tests above and the third fails outright: we cannot tell from the label, the material or any test whether a given unit has it. What is determinable is the design that permits it.

A failure inherent to the design, avoidable by stated care, and not detectable in the product as sold caps at careful, with the mitigation stated.

Not good, because the failure is unrecoverable once it happens, invisible from outside, and in a product an infant mouths. Not skip, because "skip" when "do not submerge it" solves the problem overstates what we know, and gives the reader worse information than the caveat would.

The boundary. A conditional failure alongside a named hazard is still a skip, carried by the named hazard rather than the condition. Munchkin's bath toys also harbour mould, and they are a skip because they are soft PVC with phthalate exposure when chewed — determinable from the material. Sophie's materials are sound, which is exactly why the conditional finding has to stand on its own, and on its own it is a caution.

Packaging — what touches the contents

Plastic contact is not a verdict. If it were, we would skip nearly every cosmetic and cleaning product on the market, and the flag would stop carrying information precisely because it fires on everything. Almost everything that leaches out of a plastic is lipophilic, so oil pulls it out and water largely does not. The pairing decides.

Heat moves everything one step worse, because temperature drives migration harder than any other single variable. Exposure route gives relief in the other direction: a rinse-off gets one step, and something that never touches a person — laundry, surface cleaner — gets two.

When there are no contents

Most products are not a substance in a container. A yoga mat, a toothbrush, a cutting board, a vacuum: the object is the material. For those, this check reads the surface that actually reaches a person, and the answer is the same fact as the formula check rather than a second, emptier question.

ObjectThe surface that reaches you
Cutting boardThe cut surface, because knife grooves shed it into food
Yoga matThe top layer, pressed against skin for an hour while you breathe above it
ToothbrushThe bristles, not the handle
VacuumThe exhaust, because an unsealed one returns the finest dust to the room
CookwareThe cooking surface, at temperature
Bottle or cupThe whole drink path, lid and straw included

So a stainless bowl with no coating passes both checks on one fact, and a mat whose top layer is PVC fails both on one fact. Asking twice and leaving one blank would make a sound recommendation look like it rested on a single observation.

An object immersed in what you consume is not packaging

The matrix above judges a container holding contents at rest. A tea bag is not that. It is an object put inside the drink, at near boiling temperature, and then squeezed. Reaching for the matrix produced a caution where the standard's own rules give a skip.

RuleApplied to a supermarket tea bag
Named hazardPolypropylene, from the heat sealed seam
In the ingested pathYou drink the water it steeped in
At heatWhich moves everything one step worse
Measured, not inferred11.6 billion microplastic particles per cup
The drinkware standardNo plastic in the drink path, ever. It removed the Owala picks and every straw tumbler

Five rules, one answer. Where a product is put into the thing a person swallows, read it as formula and ingestion, never as packaging.

This is the mirror of the conditional failure above, and the pair is worth holding together. Sophie la Girafe caps at careful because whether a given unit has mould is not determinable from the product. A tea bag's seam is determinable: it is a property of the bag as sold. Determinable and in the ingested path is a skip; conditional and undetectable is a caution.

Independent tests — what a lab measured

A result’s scope is set by the contamination mechanism, not by the brand. Ask how the contaminant got in and the answer tells you exactly how far the finding travels.

So: baby toothpaste tests positive for lead, does the adult version inherit it? Yes, as a caution, never a skip, and only if they share the implicated ingredient. Lead rides in on the abrasive or mineral active, which the brand buys once for the whole range. If the adult paste uses silica instead, it does not transfer at all.

An absent test is a gap, not a finding. Missing evidence is disclosed, never counted against a product that is otherwise sound.

Recalls and lawsuits — what happened

A filed suit is an allegation, so on its own it caps at caution; only a settlement or judgment reaches skip. Recalls decay: open is a skip, closed and remedied under two years is a caution, older than that is informational. Three or more distinct recalls in five years is the one route to a brand-level verdict, because at that point it is evidence about the quality system rather than any one product.

Absence of a recall is not a pass. In these categories it usually means no regulator was looking.

03

What earns a recommendation

A skip or caution may rest on inherited evidence, provided the card names the scope it came from. A good may not. Inheritance can warn; it can never recommend.

And a recommendation needs its checks done. Whatever awarded the good, a store pick, a lab result, a hand written scorecard, it ships only when formula, packaging and the legal check each carry a finding. Until then the verdict is held back, the missing checks are named, and the verdict returns on its own the moment the research lands.

Independent testing sits outside that gate on purpose. Almost nothing in these categories is independently tested, and the maker does not control whether a lab has looked, so a missing test is disclosed on the card rather than blocking the verdict. A testing finding binds in full: a failure caps the verdict like any other front, and a clean result at a stated detection limit is the strongest credential a recommendation can carry.

Held back is not a euphemism for rejected. The gate exists because five different routes could award a good and only one of them looked at the scorecard; one gate, at the end, closes them all at once. On an Amazon listing a held product says exactly what state it is in: which checks are done, which are still open, and how to be told when it clears.

Two fronts can be answered by structure rather than paperwork. For a durable good the object is its contact surface, so a stainless bowl’s packaging is the stainless and the material answers both checks. And a third-party lab result at product scope outranks a label read; it is the strongest single piece of evidence a front can hold.

A front can also come back “none”: we checked, and no evidence of that kind exists for this product, because no lab tests toothbrush handles. That is a finding, and it satisfies the gate. “Unassessed” means nobody has looked yet. It is not a finding, and it blocks the recommendation until someone looks.

A careful product may stay a published pick when it is the honest best option under a stated constraint and the caveat travels with it. The only genuinely plastic-free toothbrush bristle is boar, which is not vegan, so a nylon brush is the best available choice for anyone who needs one. Those rows are marked trade-off below.

Otherwise the answer is not reviewed, with a request-a-review button. That is a real, shippable state, and it is always better than a guess.

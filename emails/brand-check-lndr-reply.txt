Subject: Your Brand Check request: LNDR

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting LNDR through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Good choice. Safe to wear, and better documented than almost anything else in performance wear.

LNDR is a London brand founded in 2015 by three women. We judge activewear on what it is certified and tested for, because that is the part that governs what ends up in your body, and LNDR clears that bar where most technical labels do not even try.

Why we like it:

- OEKO-TEX Standard 100 on the fabrics. This is the certification that matters most for anything worn against skin. It screens finished fabric against a list of more than 1,000 harmful substances, and since 2023 the standard restricts PFAS and total organic fluorine. So even though LNDR does not publish a separate PFAS policy, the certification covers that ground.

- BPA free, and they state it on the product pages. Most activewear brands will not make that claim, and it matters, because independent testing has found BPA in leggings and sports bras from several household names.

- Some GOTS certified organic cotton in the seamless line. Where they blend, they blend with a certified organic fiber rather than more synthetic.

- No recalls, no product litigation, no regulatory actions that we could find. The only lawsuit in their history is a trademark case they brought against Nike, and won.

One honest note on the fabric:

- Most of the range is polyamide, which is nylon, and nylon sheds microplastic fibers in the wash. To be clear about what that is and is not: it is a water and environment problem, not a reason to worry about wearing it. Nothing here is leaching into you. The fix is simple and it works. Wash it in a Guppyfriend bag and you keep most of those fibers out of the water:

- One claim to read carefully. Their seamless pieces use Amni Soul Eco polyamide, marketed as biodegrading within five years in landfill conditions. That describes what happens after you throw the garment away, in a specific anaerobic environment. It is a better end of life story than ordinary nylon, but it does not mean the fabric is plastic free while you are wearing it.

What we would do: Buy it. If you want technical gear that performs and you care about what is against your skin, LNDR is one of the better documented choices in the category, and nylon is genuinely more durable than natural fiber activewear, so it will last you longer. Wash it in a Guppyfriend bag and you have covered the one real downside.

If you would rather have a natural main fiber, Tripulse is our other good pick in this category, TENCEL Lyocell with bluesign and GOTS on top of OEKO-TEX. It is less hard wearing than nylon, so it is a genuine trade off rather than a straight upgrade.

Subject: Your Brand Check request: LNDR

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting LNDR through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Careful. Cleaner chemistry than most performance brands, but it is still synthetic.

LNDR is a London brand founded in 2015 by three women, and it is genuinely one of the more thoughtful technical labels out there. It does the chemical safety part properly. What it cannot do, because no synthetic brand can, is stop the fabric from shedding plastic while you wear and wash it.

What we like:

- OEKO-TEX Standard 100 on the fabrics. That is the certification that matters most for what touches your skin. It screens finished fabric against a list of more than 1,000 harmful substances, and since 2023 the standard restricts PFAS and total organic fluorine. So while LNDR does not publish a standalone PFAS policy or its own test results, the certification covers that ground.

- BPA free, and they say it. Their product pages list BPA free alongside the OEKO-TEX mark. That is a specific claim most activewear brands do not make, and it matters, because BPA has turned up in independent testing of leggings and sports bras from several big names.

- Some GOTS organic cotton in the seamless line. Where they blend, they blend with a certified organic fiber rather than more synthetic.

- No recalls, no product litigation, no regulatory actions that we could find. The only lawsuit in their history is a trademark case they brought against Nike, and won.

Where we would slow down:

- Recycled polyamide is still nylon. Most of the range, including the Streamline and Chisel leggings, is recycled polyamide with elastane. Recycling the feedstock is a real waste benefit and we do not want to dismiss it, but the finished fabric behaves exactly like virgin nylon on your body. It sheds microplastic fibers into your wash water and into the air around you. Recycled synthetic is a waste solution, not a microplastic solution.

- The biodegradable claim describes a landfill, not your washing machine. Their seamless pieces use Amni Soul Eco polyamide, 96% with 4% elastane, tested to ASTM D5511 and ASTM D6691 and marketed as biodegrading within five years in landfill conditions. Read that carefully. It is a statement about what happens after you throw the garment away, in a specific anaerobic environment. It changes nothing about shedding during the years you actually wear it, the elastane does not biodegrade at all, and nylon breaking down in a landfill releases methane. It is a better end of life story than ordinary nylon. It is not a reason to treat the fabric as plastic free.

- Performance wear is the highest shed category there is. Tight, stretchy, high friction fabric worn while you sweat and then washed often is close to the worst case for fiber release. That is true of every brand in this category, LNDR included.

What we would do: If you want technical performance gear and your priority is avoiding PFAS and BPA against your skin, LNDR is a defensible choice and better documented than most of its competitors. If your priority is reducing the plastic you shed and breathe, no synthetic brand clears that bar, and you would want organic cotton, hemp, or TENCEL instead. Brands we would point you to there are 1 People, Boldwill, Tripulse, and Jungmaven, all of which passed our No Plastic Test.

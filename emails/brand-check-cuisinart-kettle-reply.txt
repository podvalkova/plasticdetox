Subject: Your Brand Check request: Cuisinart PerfecTemp Electric Kettle

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting the Cuisinart PerfecTemp 1.7 Liter Electric Kettle through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Skip.

The good news: the kettle body is 304 stainless steel and Cuisinart lists it as BPA free, so it is a step up from the all plastic kettles on the market.

Why we still say skip:

- Plastic in the boiling water path. Even though most of the interior is stainless, the lid interior, the hinge, the mesh filter at the spout, and the water level window are all plastic, and they sit right in the boiling water every time you use it. Boiling water is the most aggressive condition for pulling microplastics and additives out of plastic, so for us this is the whole ballgame. BPA free does not mean plastic free, and heated plastic can still shed microplastics and release other bisphenols.

- The plastic smell. A fair number of owners report a plastic or chemical smell on the first several boils, which is the plastic components off gassing.

- Recalls. To be clear, there is no CPSC safety recall on this kettle. The lid button sticking that you may see online is a common malfunction complaint, not a formal recall. Worth knowing though: Cuisinart as a brand carries a very large 2016 food processor recall over blades that could break off into food, so their recall history is not spotless.

What we would use instead:

- Best value, plastic free water path: the Cosori glass or stainless steel electric kettle. The water only touches borosilicate glass or stainless steel and the plastic parts stay out of the water. It is 1.7 liters like the Cuisinart and usually a good bit cheaper.

- Premium pick: the Fellow Stagg EKG. It has a verified 304 stainless steel water path, and the only non metal part touching the water is an inert food grade silicone gasket. It is pricier and smaller at 0.9 liters, but it is the gold standard if you want zero plastic in the water.

Either way, the goal is simple: keep plastic out of the part that holds the boiling water.

Hope that helps you make the call.

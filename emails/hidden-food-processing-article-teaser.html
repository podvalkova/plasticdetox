The bag said raw. It wasn't. Almost every almond you buy in the US has been steamed or fumigated before it reached the shelf, and it can still legally be sold as raw.

That is the pattern in our new guide. The words raw, natural, and fresh routinely hide one processing step, and once you know which step belongs to which food, you can buy around it. A genuinely cleaner version almost always exists. The catch is that it is specific to each food, not one blanket rule.

We go food by food, from the swaps worth real effort to the popular fixes that are oversold, and end with a one page cheat sheet you can screenshot for the grocery store.

A few things that surprised us:

Some decaf is washed with the same solvent found in paint strippers, and two words on the bag skip it.

Tuna that is too bright and too even a red can be a gas treatment masking a fish that has aged.

And some popular fixes are oversold. Farmed salmon is not injected with dye, and switching to pink salt does not solve microplastics.

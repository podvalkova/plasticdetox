Most drip coffee makers run near boiling water through plastic every morning. The tank is plastic. The tubing is plastic. The basket the water sprays through is usually plastic too.

That matters because heat pulls particles out of plastic. In a Nature Food study, heated polypropylene released up to 16 million microplastic particles per liter. That was at 70°C. Your coffee brews hotter.

So we looked for brewers that pass one rule: nothing but steel, ceramic, or glass between the water and your cup. The good news is that the cleanest options are old, simple designs.

1. Replace the drip machine. The Farberware Yosemite percolator makes 8 cups on the stove, hands off, through an all stainless path.

2. Keep the French press. Our top pick, the Bodum Chambord, brews through nothing but glass and stainless steel.

3. Pull espresso with a lever. The Flair PRO 3 has an all stainless brew head and no water tank at all. Manual levers were the only espresso design we found that keeps plastic out of the shot.

Then there are the beans, which matter as much as the brewer. Coffee can carry ochratoxin A, a toxin made by mold. Europe caps it in roasted coffee. The US sets no limit at all. Many decafs are also made with methylene chloride, a solvent the EPA banned for most uses in 2024. The FDA still allows it in coffee. So choose organic beans, look for mold testing, and pick Swiss Water decaf. Our coffee guide shows how to pick clean beans.

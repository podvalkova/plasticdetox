Subject: Your Brand Check request: Hydrapeak

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting Hydrapeak through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Careful. Not because we found something wrong with it, but because the brand will not answer the question that matters most.

Every double walled bottle has to be vacuum sealed, and that seal leaves a small dot on the underside of the base, hidden under a cap. On a lot of brands that dot is lead or a lead alloy at around 600,000 ppm. It is why the Stanley story blew up. It is the single most important fact about any insulated bottle.

Hydrapeak does not address it anywhere: not in their FAQ, not on their product pages, not in any published lab report. They have never been independently XRF tested that we can find, and they are absent from Lead Safe Mama's list of confirmed lead free bottles. That is silence rather than evidence of lead, and their bottle may well be fine. But this is something you drink from daily, and we will not fill in that blank for a brand.

What checks out: 18/8 food grade stainless with a copper lined vacuum layer, free from BPA, BPS and BPF, and no recalls or lawsuits we could find.

The other flag: the straw, chug and bite lids are plastic and sit in the drink path, so you sip through plastic every time. Hydrapeak does not say which polymer. Keep hot liquid out of them.

What we would do: If you are buying new, get a bottle where the construction is verified. Klean Kanteen Classic or a Hydro Flask wide mouth both let you drink from a bare steel rim instead of a plastic spout, and both are confirmed lead free.

If you already own a Hydrapeak, no need to throw it out. Even on bottles that do use a leaded dot, it sits under the base cap and never touches your drink. Keep that cap intact, do not pry or sand at the base, and drink from the rim rather than the straw lid where you can.

Hope that helps you make the call.

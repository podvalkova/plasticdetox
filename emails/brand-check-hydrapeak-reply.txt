Subject: Your Brand Check request: Hydrapeak

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting Hydrapeak through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Careful. Not because we found something wrong with it, but because the brand will not answer the one question that matters most.

Here is the thing to understand about insulated stainless bottles, because it is not obvious and almost nobody explains it. A double walled bottle has to be vacuum sealed, and that seal leaves a small dot on the underside of the base, usually hidden under a plastic or steel cap. On a large number of brands, that dot is lead or a lead alloy running around 600,000 ppm. It is the reason the Stanley story blew up a couple of years ago. It is the single most important fact about any bottle in this category.

Hydrapeak does not address it. Not in their FAQ, not on their product pages, not in any published lab report. They have never been independently XRF tested that we can find, and they are not on Lead Safe Mama's list of confirmed lead free insulated bottles, which currently covers Hydro Flask and Owala.

We want to be fair about what that means: it is silence, not evidence of lead. Their bottle may well be lead free. But this is a container you drink out of every day, and we are not willing to fill in that blank on a brand's behalf.

What checks out:

- 18/8 food grade stainless steel body with a copper lined vacuum layer. That is the right material, and it is what the good bottles use.

- Free from BPA, BPS and BPF, which they state directly.

- No recalls, no lawsuits, no CPSC complaints that we could find.

Where we would slow down:

- The lead question above, which is the main one.

- The straw, chug and bite lids are plastic and they sit in the drink path, so you are sipping through plastic every time. Hydrapeak does not say which polymer it is. Do not run hot liquid through those lids.

What we would do: If you are buying new, we would pick a bottle where the construction is actually verified. Klean Kanteen Classic or a Hydro Flask wide mouth both let you drink from a bare steel rim rather than through a plastic spout, and both are confirmed lead free.

If you already own a Hydrapeak, there is no reason to panic or throw it out. Even on bottles that do use a leaded sealing dot, it sits under the base cap and never touches your drink. Keep that cap intact, do not pry or sand at the base, and use the bare rim rather than the straw lid where you can.

One thing you can do that we would genuinely like the answer to: email Hydrapeak and ask for a lead test report on the sealing dot. A brand that has one will send it. If they send you anything, forward it to us and we will update their rating publicly.

Hope that helps you make the call.

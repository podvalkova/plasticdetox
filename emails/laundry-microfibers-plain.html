Last week we told you detergent does not stop microplastic fibers leaving your machine. A few of you replied asking what does. Here is the honest answer, ranked by how much plastic each fix actually catches.

First, the scale. One load of synthetic clothing sheds 700,000 to 12 million plastic fibers. Treatment plants miss a large share, which is how those fibers end up in rivers, sea salt, and tap water.

1. Change the settings. Free. Cold water cuts shedding by about 30%. Shorter cycles, full loads, and the gentle setting each shave off more. Your clothes last longer too.

2. Wash synthetics in a Guppyfriend bag. Zip them inside before the wash. Independent testing shows it catches 54% of fibers, and clothes shed less inside it in the first place. Scrape the lint into the trash, never down the drain. About $35.

3. Fit an external filter. The gold standard. The Filtrol 160 attaches to your machine's discharge hose and catches 80 to 90% of fibers. France now requires fiber filters on every new washing machine, which tells you where this is going.

One more thing while we are here. Your dryer is the forgotten half of this. A single cycle can vent up to 120 million fibers straight into the air. Air dry your synthetics when you can.

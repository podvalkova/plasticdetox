Subject: Your Brand Check request: Indigo Luna

Hi there,

Thanks for requesting Indigo Luna through Brand Check. Here is what our review turned up.

Our verdict: Careful. It depends entirely on which piece you buy.

Indigo Luna is a small Bali based label with a genuinely good natural fabric range and a synthetic range sitting right next to it under the same eco branding. The difference matters more than the brand name does.

What we like:

- The natural range is the real thing. The Ananda yoga line is GOTS certified organic cotton, and their TENCEL Modal and Lenzing ECOVERO pieces come from closed loop wood pulp processes rather than conventional viscose chemistry. Their fabrics page lists OEKO-TEX Standard 100 across the range, which screens finished fabric for azo dyes, formaldehyde, and heavy metals.

- Plant dyes, not synthetic. The Eucalyptus Intimates are dyed with 100% plant dyes, and dyeing is done by hand in their own Bali facility rather than jobbed out to a mill with unknown wastewater.

- Small batch and transparent about where things are made. Cut, sewn, and dyed in a women run factory in Bali, in small runs. No lawsuits, recalls, or regulatory actions that we could find.

Where we would slow down:

- The leggings and swimwear are ECONYL, which is still nylon. ECONYL is regenerated from discarded fishing nets and other nylon waste, which is a real waste story, but once the fabric exists it behaves like any other synthetic. It sheds microplastics in the wash and on your skin, and it is not plastic free. If you are here to reduce plastic against your body and in your water, the ECONYL leggings and swim pieces do not do that. Recycled synthetic is a waste solution, not a microplastic solution.

- Bamboo viscose is in the mix too. Some styles are 96% bamboo viscose with 4% spandex. Conventional bamboo viscose is made with carbon disulfide, a process we do not count as clean no matter how soft the result. This is different from their TENCEL and ECOVERO, which use closed loop solvent recovery. The tag will tell you which one you are getting.

- Almost every stretch fabric has spandex. Their blends run 4% to 10% spandex or elastane, which is synthetic and sheds. That is normal for anything with stretch, and it is a far smaller fraction than a full nylon legging, but it is not zero.

- One certification inconsistency. Marketing describes the yoga range as GOTS certified organic cotton, while the fabrics page lists only OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for cotton. Those are different standards. OEKO-TEX tests the finished fabric for harmful substances, GOTS covers organic fiber content plus processing and social criteria. If GOTS is what you are paying for, ask them for the certificate number for the specific style.

What we would do: Buy the Ananda organic cotton pieces and the TENCEL Modal and ECOVERO styles, and treat those as a genuinely good swap for conventional activewear. Skip the ECONYL leggings and swimwear, and skip the bamboo viscose styles. If you want stretch leggings with no synthetic shedding at all, that is a hard category, and the honest answer is that organic cotton with a few percent elastane is currently the cleanest option available anywhere, including here.

If you already own the ECONYL pieces, wash them in a Guppyfriend bag and they will shed far less. No need to throw anything out.

Hope that helps you make the call.

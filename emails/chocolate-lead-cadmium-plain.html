Consumer Reports tested 28 dark chocolate bars for lead and cadmium. All 28 had both. Twenty three broke California's own legal threshold, the one that requires a warning label by law.

The two metals get in differently. Cadmium comes from the soil as the tree grows. Lead lands on the bean's outside while it dries in the open air, picked up from dust along roads. Organic did not help: Alter Eco and Theo, both certified organic, were among the worst results.

Chocolate is close to half fat, the worst pairing for plastic contact. A 2016 German lab test found mineral oil in 19 of 20 chocolates, traced to the wrapper. A hot cocoa K-Cup pushes near boiling water through a sealed plastic capsule, shown to shed billions of nanoplastic particles per cup.

Fewer daily squares, milk over dark when you can, and skip the pod: loose cocoa powder and a kettle work just as well. If buying a bar, Mast's 80% Cocoa tested lowest of all 28, still a real, detected result, not a clean pass.

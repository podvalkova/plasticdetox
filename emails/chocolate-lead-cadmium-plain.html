Consumer Reports tested 28 dark chocolate bars for lead and cadmium. All 28 had both metals. Twenty three broke California's own legal threshold, the one that by law requires a warning label, not just a concerning reading in someone's press release.

The two metals get in differently, which is part of why nothing fully escapes. Cadmium comes up through the soil as the cacao tree grows, so it is baked in before harvest. Lead lands on the outside of the bean while it dries in the open air for days, picked up from dust and dirt, often along roads. Organic did not help either. Several of the worst results, including Alter Eco and Theo, were organic certified.

Then we asked the question this site actually exists for: what about the wrapper? Chocolate is close to half fat, the single worst pairing there is for plastic contact. A 2016 German lab test found mineral oil residue in 19 of 20 chocolates, traced back to the wrapper itself. And if hot cocoa in your house comes from a K-Cup, you are pushing near boiling water through a sealed plastic capsule, the same setup already shown to shed billions of nanoplastic particles per cup when it is coffee inside.

So: fewer daily squares, milk chocolate over dark when you can, and skip the pod, loose cocoa powder and a kettle does the same job. If you are buying a bar anyway, Mast's 80% Cocoa tested lowest of all 28 on both metals, though we want to be honest that it is still a real, detected result, not a clean pass.

Oral Care

Three products touch your mouth every single day, and recent research has taken a hard look at each one. The findings are not comforting.

A 2019 study tied a popular floss to higher PFAS in blood. Independent testing keeps finding nylon bristles in brushes sold as "bamboo." And many toothpastes still use additives restricted as foods elsewhere. Here is what the science says, plus our pick for each.

What the research says

1. Toothbrush

Independent testing keeps finding nylon bristles in brushes sold as "bamboo," and no brand has proven a fully biodegradable bristle. The honest picks are natural boar bristle or a recyclable head electric.

Boar bristle → Electric (SURI) →

2. Floss

The "glide" in most floss is PTFE, the Teflon family. A 2019 study tied Oral-B Glide to higher PFAS in blood, and 2024 testing clocked it at a record fluorine level. Silk floss is the only kind that is both PFAS free and plastic free.

Dental Lace (silk) → Cocofloss (vegan) →

3. Toothpaste

Many tubes still use plastic microbeads, artificial dyes, and whiteners restricted elsewhere. We read the labels and found the cleanest verified picks.

Weleda Salt → Boka (hydroxyapatite) →

Start with whichever one bugs you most. Small swaps, made once, that you use every day.

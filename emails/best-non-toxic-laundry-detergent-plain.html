If you switched to laundry sheets to get away from the plastic jug, I have some annoying news. The sheet itself is held together with polyvinyl alcohol, a synthetic polymer made from petroleum feedstock. Same for detergent pods. Brands call it plastic free because it dissolves, but dissolving and degrading are not the same thing. Sugar dissolves too.

I want to be fair about how unresolved this is. The main study arguing PVA survives wastewater treatment modeled the numbers rather than measuring them, and when Blueland petitioned the EPA to require real safety testing in 2023, the EPA turned it down for exactly that reason. So nobody has proven harm here. Nobody has proven safety either. My view is simple: when a product's whole pitch is the absence of plastic, the burden sits with the product, and a powder in a cardboard box does not have this argument at all.

The bigger problem is in the mainstream aisle. 1,4-dioxane is a probable human carcinogen that forms as a manufacturing byproduct, which means no labeling law requires it to be disclosed. Testing reported through New York's rulemaking found Arm and Hammer Clean Burst at 4.28 ppm and Tide Original at 3.67 ppm, against a state limit of 1 ppm. Those brands say they reformulated before the deadline, and I believe them, but the chemistry that created the problem is still the basis of those formulas.

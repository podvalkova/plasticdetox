If you switched to laundry sheets to get away from the plastic jug, here is the annoying part. The sheet itself is held together with PVA, a plastic made from petroleum. Same for pods. Brands call it plastic free because it dissolves. But dissolving is not the same as breaking down. Sugar dissolves too.

To be fair, nobody has proven PVA does harm. Nobody has proven it is safe either. A powder in a cardboard box skips the argument entirely.

The bigger problem is the mainstream aisle. 1,4-dioxane is a probable carcinogen. It forms during manufacturing, so it never has to appear on a label. Testing found Arm and Hammer Clean Burst at 4.28 ppm and Tide Original at 3.67, against New York's limit of 1.

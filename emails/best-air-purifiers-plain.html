Most of the microplastics conversation is about what you eat and drink. The route people forget is the one that runs all day: the air in your home. Indoor air can hold many times the microplastic load of outdoor air, because your rooms are full of the things that shed it, synthetic clothing, carpet, upholstery, curtains and the dust that collects it all. You breathe roughly 15,000 liters of that air every day, and the bedroom is where it concentrates, since you spend a third of your life there breathing inches from fabric.

The good news: this is one of the easiest exposures to actually cut. A sealed True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns, and it catches the bigger plastic fibers and the smaller nanoplastics even more reliably than that. One quiet machine running all night in the bedroom does more for what you inhale than almost anything else you can buy. Two warnings before you shop, though. Skip anything with a built in ionizer or ozone function, which adds a lung irritant to your air. And skip the cheap no name marketplace units: one of them was recalled this February, about 191,000 units, after 37 overheating reports and a fire.
